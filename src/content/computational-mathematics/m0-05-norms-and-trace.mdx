# Matrix Norms and the Trace

How big is a matrix? Its number of rows doesn't answer that, because a 3-by-3 matrix of tiny values and one of huge values are the same size. A **norm** reduces a matrix to one non-negative number that measures the magnitude of its entries.

## What every norm promises

There are several norms, but all the ones here share these properties, for any scalar $k$:

1. $\|A\| \ge 0$
2. $\|A\| = 0$ only when $A$ is the zero matrix
3. $\|kA\| = |k|\,\|A\|$
4. $\|A + B\| \le \|A\| + \|B\|$, the triangle inequality
5. $\|AB\| \le \|A\|\,\|B\|$

## Three norms to know

The **1-norm** is the largest absolute column sum:

$$
\|A\|_1 = \max_{j} \sum_{i=1}^{n} |a_{ij}|
$$

The **infinity-norm** is the largest absolute row sum:

$$
\|A\|_\infty = \max_{i} \sum_{j=1}^{n} |a_{ij}|
$$

A handy way to remember them: "1" is tall and thin like a column, and "∞" is short and wide like a row.

The **Euclidean norm** is the square root of the sum of every entry squared. It's Pythagoras applied to the whole matrix:

$$
\|A\|_E = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}^2}
$$

## The trace

The **trace** of a square matrix is the sum of its main diagonal:

$$
\operatorname{tr}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ii} = a_{11} + a_{22} + \cdots + a_{nn}
$$

For `magic(4)`, `trace` returns $16 + 11 + 6 + 1 = 34$, which is also what every row and column of that matrix adds up to.
