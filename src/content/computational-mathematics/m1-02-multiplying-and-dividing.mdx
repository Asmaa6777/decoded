# Multiplying and Dividing Polynomials

Multiply two polynomials by hand and you collect like terms. Do that on coefficient vectors and you've performed a **convolution**, an operation that also runs image filters and audio effects.

## Convolution

Take $a(s) = s^2 + 2s + 3$ and $b(s) = 4s^2 + 5s + 6$. The coefficient of $s^n$ in the product comes from every pair of powers adding up to $n$:

$$
c_n = \sum_{i} a_i \, b_{n-i}
$$

Picture $b$ flipped and sliding past $a$. Wherever they overlap, multiply the pairs and add:

<ConvolutionVisualizer />

```matlab
a = [1 2 3];  b = [4 5 6];
c = conv(a, b)

c =
     4    13    28    27    18
```

So the product is $4s^4 + 13s^3 + 28s^2 + 27s + 18$. If $a$ has degree $m$ and $b$ has degree $n$, the result has degree $m + n$.

## Deconvolution

`deconv` runs this backwards, which is polynomial long division. It returns the quotient and the remainder:

```matlab
[q, r] = deconv(c, a)

q =
     4     5     6
r =
     0     0     0     0     0
```

A remainder of all zeros tells you that $a$ divides $c$ exactly. This also gives a quick test for a root: $r$ is a root of $p$ exactly when `deconv(p, [1 -r])` leaves no remainder.
