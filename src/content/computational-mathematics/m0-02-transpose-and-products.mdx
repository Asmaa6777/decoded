# Transpose and Vector Products

Flipping a matrix over its diagonal looks like a small trick, but it's what lets you multiply two vectors in both possible orders, and those two orders give very different answers.

## The transpose

The **transpose** of an $m$-by-$n$ matrix $A$ is the $n$-by-$m$ matrix $A'$ whose rows are the columns of $A$:

$$
(A')_{ij} = a_{ji}
$$

Press the button and watch where each number goes. Entries on the main diagonal have $i = j$, so they stay where they are. Everything else swaps with its mirror image:

<TransposeVisualizer />

MATLAB writes the transpose with an apostrophe:

```matlab
B = magic(3);
B'

ans =
     8     3     4
     1     5     9
     6     7     2
```

A matrix is **symmetric** when $A' = A$. `pascal(3)` is symmetric and `magic(3)` isn't. Transposing also turns a row vector into a column vector, which is the move you need next.

<Callout tone="sky" label="For complex numbers">
In MATLAB, `'` also conjugates complex entries. Use `.'` when you want the transpose alone. For real matrices the two are identical.
</Callout>

## The dot product

For two vectors of the same length, the **dot product** (or inner product) multiplies matching entries and adds the results:

$$
a \cdot b = a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 + \cdots + a_n b_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i b_i
$$

For example, with $u = (1, -2, 3, 4)$ and $v = (2, 3, -2, 1)$:

$$
u \cdot v = (1)(2) + (-2)(3) + (3)(-2) + (4)(1) = -6
$$

## One pair of vectors, two products

Take a column vector $u$ and a row vector $v$ of the same length. Both orders are legal matrix products, because the inner sizes match either way, yet the results have nothing in common:

- `v * u` is 1-by-3 times 3-by-1, so the result is 1-by-1. This is the **inner product**.
- `u * v` is 3-by-1 times 1-by-3, so the result is 3-by-3. This is the **outer product**.

Switch between the two and compare:

<VectorProductVisualizer />

If `x` and `y` are both column vectors, then `x * y` is an error because the inner sizes are n and 1. Transpose one of them and it works: `x' * y` is the inner product and `x * y'` is the outer product.

## Where you'll meet this

<PracticalApplication title="Finding similar documents" tool="Search, embeddings">
Text embeddings are long vectors. A vector database ranks results using dot products, usually of normalised vectors, which gives cosine similarity.
</PracticalApplication>
