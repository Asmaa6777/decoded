# The Flags Register

After you work out a sum, you might notice things about the answer: "that's zero" or "that's negative". The CPU does the same, automatically, after each arithmetic or logic instruction. It records what it noticed in single-bit **flags**, gathered in a 16-bit register. Only 9 of those bits are used.

Here's why it matters. There's no `if` statement in hardware. When you write `if (a == b)`, the CPU subtracts, checks whether the Zero flag got set, and then decides whether to jump. All decision-making comes down to flags.

## Carry and overflow

These two confuse nearly everyone at first, and the reason is that the bits `11001000` could be 200 or -56, depending on how you read them. The CPU doesn't know which you mean, so it reports on both, and you choose which flag to check:

- **Treating numbers as unsigned**: check **CF**. 200 + 100 = 300 doesn't fit in 8 bits, so CF is 1.
- **Treating numbers as signed**: check **OF**. 100 + 50 = 150, but the largest signed 8-bit value is 127. The result reads as -106, so OF is 1.

An easy way to spot overflow: adding two positives gives a negative, or adding two negatives gives a positive. If you need a refresher on why, see [the two's complement circle](/courses/logic-design/binary-arithmetic).

## Control flags: how to behave

The other three are settings you control, and they tell the CPU how to act:

| Flag | Name | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **TF** | Trap | Stop after every instruction. Debuggers use it for single-stepping |
| **IF** | Interrupt | 1 means the CPU accepts [interrupts](/courses/microprocessors/interrupts), and 0 means it ignores maskable ones |
| **DF** | Direction | String instructions move forward (0) or backward (1) through memory |

You set them with instructions like `STI`/`CLI` (set and clear IF) and `STD`/`CLD` (set and clear DF). `STC`, `CLC` and `CMC` set, clear and flip the carry flag.

<Callout tone="orange" label="Bit vocabulary">
In a byte, the **MSB** (most significant bit) is on the far left and the **LSB** (least significant bit) on the far right. The upper four bits are the upper **nibble** and the lower four are the lower nibble. The auxiliary flag watches for a carry between those nibbles.
</Callout>
