# Karnaugh Maps

Here is the key fact behind this whole lesson. Take the two terms $AB'C$ and $AB'C'$. They differ only in $C$, so factor it out: $AB'(C + C') = AB'$. Whatever $C$ is, the answer is the same, so $C$ doesn't matter and we can delete it.

To simplify, then, you want pairs of terms that differ in exactly one variable. In a truth table those pairs are scattered about. A **Karnaugh map** (K-map) rearranges the table into a grid so that those pairs sit next to each other, and then your eyes can do the algebra.

## Reading the map

Each cell is one row of the truth table. The row and column labels run 00, 01, **11**, 10. That's [Gray code](/courses/logic-design/codes-and-floating-point), not counting order, and it's what makes the map work: any two neighbouring cells differ by exactly one bit.

The map also wraps around. The left edge touches the right edge, and on a 4-variable map the top touches the bottom, as if it were drawn on a doughnut.

Click cells to turn them on and off. The map finds the groups for you and shows the shrunken expression.

This starting map is $AB'C + A'BC + A'B'C + A'B'C' + AB'C'$. The answer is $B' + A'C$: from five terms to two.

## Grouping rules

1. Groups must contain 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 cells: a power of 2.
2. Groups are rectangles, and they can wrap around the edges.
3. Make each group as big as you can. Bigger groups mean shorter terms.
4. Every 1 must be in at least one group, and groups may overlap.
5. Use as few groups as you can.

## Reading the answer

For each group, look at which variables stay the same across all its cells. Keep those and drop the rest:

- A variable that's always 1 in the group appears plain.
- A variable that's always 0 in the group appears inverted.
- A variable that changes inside the group cancels out.

On a 3-variable map, a group of 1 gives a 3-variable term, a group of 2 gives 2 variables, a group of 4 gives 1 variable and a group of all 8 gives just 1.

Software simplifies today's circuits, and it can handle thousands of inputs. K-maps are how you learn what that software is doing, and they're still the quickest method for a small function by hand. An exam will almost certainly ask you for one.
