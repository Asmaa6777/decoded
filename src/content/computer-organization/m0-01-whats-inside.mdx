# What's Inside a Computer

Imagine doing a long sum on paper. Your paper holds the instructions and the numbers. Your brain reads the next step, does the calculation and writes down the result. A computer is built the same way, with electronic parts for each role.

| You with paper | In a computer | Its job |
|---|---|---|
| Paper | Memory | Stores instructions and data |
| The part of your brain that follows the steps | Control unit | Reads each instruction and directs the work |
| The part of your brain that calculates | ALU (arithmetic logic unit) | Does the arithmetic and comparisons |
| Eyes and hand | Input and output | Gets information in and out |

The control unit and ALU together are the **CPU** (central processing unit). A CPU built on a single chip is called a **microprocessor**.

## The five units

Every computer, from a phone to a data centre, has the same five parts:

1. **CPU**: follows instructions and does the calculating.
2. **Memory**: holds the program that's running and the data it's using.
3. **Input**: brings data in.
4. **Output**: sends results out.
5. **Bus**: the shared wires connecting everything.

## The idea that makes it all work

The most important idea in this course is the **stored program**: instructions are kept in memory, in the same form as data, as numbers. To make the machine do something different, you load different numbers. You don't rewire anything.

That's why one piece of hardware can be a word processor one minute and a game the next. The computers we use today are called **Von Neumann machines** after the mathematician credited with this design.

<Callout tone="sky" label="Hardware and software">
**Hardware** is every physical part. **Software** is the instructions, also called programs. A computer processes **data** (raw facts) into **information** (results that mean something to a person), and it doesn't understand what either one means.
</Callout>
